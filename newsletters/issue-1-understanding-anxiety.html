Understanding Anxiety

A therapist's honest breakdown of what anxiety actually is — and how to work with your body to disarm it.

 

“She didn't come to me because she had anxiety. She came because she hadn't slept through the night in three years.”

Her name was Sara. Forty-one years old, two kids in middle school, a job she was good at, a husband she loved. From the outside, everything looked fine. But every morning, her chest was tight before her feet hit the floor. She'd snap at her kids over nothing — a backpack left by the door, a forgotten permission slip — and then spend the next hour drowning in guilt.

She'd been to her doctor. Blood work came back clean. “It's just stress,” they told her. She believed them. For three years, she believed them, carrying the silent weight of her symptoms alone.

“I thought I was just bad at handling life,” she told me in our first session. “I didn't realize that what I was experiencing had a name.”

 

What's actually happening in your body

Here's what most people never get told: Anxiety isn't a personality flaw. It's not a weakness, and it's certainly not you being dramatic. It is your nervous system doing exactly what it was biologically designed to do — just activated in the wrong context.

Your brain has one job above everything else: keep you alive. It does that through an ancient security center called the amygdala — your built-in threat detector. When it senses danger, it fires instantly. Adrenaline floods your system, your heart rate accelerates, your chest tightens, and your mind races with worst-case scenarios.

This hyper-reactive threat response saved our ancestors from physical predators. The evolutionary glitch is that your modern brain cannot tell the difference between a charging wild animal and a critical email from your supervisor.

For most people struggling with chronic anxiety, that biological alarm system has simply gotten stuck in the “on” position. Not because you are broken, but because your body worked too well at protecting you for too long.

Four things that actually help

Disarming a hyperactive nervous system requires somatic intervention. Four clinically validated tools to step out of the loop and into the pause:

1. Name it out loud.

Neuroimaging shows that naming an emotion — “I am feeling anxious right now” — instantly dampens reactivity in the amygdala. It signals your prefrontal cortex to take back control.

 

2. Change your exhale.

Inhale for 4 counts, then hold and exhale slowly for 8 counts. Extending the exhale directly stimulates the vagus nerve, initiating your body's built-in calm switch.

 

3. Ground yourself.

Anxiety only lives in the future. Anchor yourself in the present with 5-4-3-2-1: five things you see, four you can touch, three you hear, two you smell, one you taste.

 

4. Stop trying to make it stop.

The struggle against anxiety is what fuels the loop. Observe the sensations mindfully: “My chest is tight, and that is okay. I am safe, and this will pass.”

 

When tools aren't enough

Sara graduated from therapy six months after we started our work together. She still experiences challenging, high-stress days. But she sleeps peacefully through the night now. She doesn't spend her mornings drowning in guilt. She understands exactly what is happening inside her body — and she knows exactly what to do with it.

“I finally feel like myself again,” she shared with me during our final session.

That is precisely what this therapeutic work is for.
