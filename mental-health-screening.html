The Clinical Tools

The same screening used in doctors' offices and therapy practices worldwide.

This isn't a custom quiz invented for a website. It uses two established, peer-reviewed instruments that have been validated across millions of patients.

Depression

PHQ-9

The Patient Health Questionnaire-9 measures the nine core symptoms of depression as defined by the DSM. It asks about mood, energy, sleep, concentration, appetite, and more — all over the past two weeks. Scores fall into five ranges: minimal, mild, moderate, moderately severe, and severe.

Developed at Pfizer in collaboration with Drs. Spitzer, Williams, and Kroenke. Published in JAMA Internal Medicine. Used in over 10,000 peer-reviewed studies.

Anxiety

GAD-7

The Generalized Anxiety Disorder 7-item scale measures the core symptoms of anxiety — worry, restlessness, irritability, difficulty concentrating, and physical tension. Like the PHQ-9, scores are placed into severity ranges that guide clinical decision-making.

Developed by the same research group. Published in the Archives of Internal Medicine. Routinely used in primary care, psychiatry, and therapy practices globally.

Before You Start

A few questions answered.

Is this a real clinical assessment?

Yes — it uses the PHQ-9 (Patient Health Questionnaire-9) for depression and the GAD-7 (Generalized Anxiety Disorder-7) for anxiety. These are validated, peer-reviewed screening tools used routinely by primary care physicians, psychiatrists, and therapists. They are not a diagnosis, but they are the same instruments used to start clinical conversations about mental health.

Who can see my answers?

Your responses are private. The questions and scores are processed in your browser. Your name and email are used to display your results and send you a copy — they are not sold, shared, or used for marketing. David does not see your individual question responses.

Does a high score mean I have a disorder?

No. A screening tool measures symptom severity — it does not diagnose. A high score means your symptoms are significant enough that talking to a mental health professional is worth considering. It's a signal, not a verdict. Many people with moderate or even severe scores on a screening are functioning well on the outside — which is exactly why the context matters.

What if my score is really low?

Low scores are good news, and the results will say so plainly. You may still find it useful to talk to someone if something doesn't feel right in your life — not everything that's worth working on shows up in an anxiety or depression screen.

What happens after I see my results?

Nothing happens automatically. You'll see a clear explanation of your scores, and if it makes sense, an option to book a free 15-minute consultation with David. There's no pressure, no follow-up email campaign, and no obligation. The screening is useful whether or not you decide to move forward with therapy.

Is this covered by insurance?

The screening itself is completely free. If you decide to move into therapy, Therapy by David accepts most major insurance plans including Aetna, Cigna, TRICARE, Medicare, Medicaid, UnitedHealthcare, and more. Coverage for the consultation can be verified when you book.
